RULING OF THE COURT
Defendant Reid moves to suppress statements made to FBI agents on December

12 15, 2015 on the ground that her statements were not made voluntarily. Reid contends
13 that she cooperated with agents out of fear that agents could cause further separation
14 from her daughter. Reid asserts that she feared for the well-being and safety of her
15 daughter. The Government contends that the totality of the circumstances supports the
16 conclusion that Reid was not in custody at any point during the interview and that there
17 are no facts to support her claim that the interview was not voluntary
18

“In evaluating voluntariness, the test is whether, considering the totality of the

19 circumstances, the government obtained the statement by physical or psychological
20 coercion or by improper inducement so that the suspect’s will was overborne.” United
21 States v. Male Juvenile, 280 F.3d 1008, 1022 (9th Cir. 2002)(internal quotations
22 omitted). In this case, the agents informed Reid that the interview was voluntary and
23 offered to conduct the interview at a later time. The evidence conclusively shows that
24 the demeanor of the agents and Reid remained calm throughout the interview. There
25 is no evidence that voices were raised, that the agents pressured Reid, or that threats or
26 false promises of any kind were made by the agents. The transcript clearly indicates
27 that Reid voluntarily answered the questions of the agents with no intimidation or
28 coercion. There are no statements from the recorded interview to support Reid’s

1 assertion that she feared for the well-being and safety of her daughter or that she feared
2 that the agents would separate her from her daughter. There are no facts before this
3 Court to support the conclusion that the Reid’s will was overborne by intimidation,
4 coercion, or deception. Based upon this record, considering the totality of the
5 circumstances, the Court concludes that the Government has carried its burden to
6 establish by a preponderance of the evidence that Reid’s statements were the voluntary.
7

The Fifth Amendment provides that “[n]o person . . . shall be compelled in any

8 criminal case to be a witness against himself . . . .” U.S. Const. Amend. V. Before any
9 interrogation that occurs in a custodial setting, a suspect must be advised of his or
10 her Fifth Amendment rights as required by Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436, 444–45
11 (1966). To determine whether a suspect is in custody, the court examines the totality
12 of the circumstances surrounding the questioning. See Thompson v. Keohane, 516 U.S.
13 99, 112 (1995). In United States v. Craighead, 539 F.3d 1073, 1083(9th Cir. 2008), the
14 Court of Appeals held that the “benchmark for custodial interrogations in locations
15 outside of the police station” is whether or not the interrogation occurred in a
16 “police-dominated atmosphere.” Id. The Court of Appeals identified the following four
17 relevant factors to determine whether the circumstances of the interrogation effected a
18 police-dominated atmosphere: “(1) the number of law enforcement personnel and
19 whether they were armed; (2) whether the suspect was at any point restrained, either by
20 physical force or by threats; (3) whether the suspect was isolated from others; and (4)
21 whether the suspect was informed that he was free to leave or terminate the interview,
22 and the context in which any such statements were made.” Id. at 1084.
23

In this case, there were four armed agents who came to the residence to execute

24 a search warrant for a cell phone. There is no evidence that the agents drew their
25 weapons at any point. There is evidence that the agents covered their weapons from the
26 sight of Reid’s daughter. After concluding that the cell phone was not in the residence,
27 the agents did not search the residence. However, the agents did ask Reid if she would
28 voluntarily answer questions about their criminal investigation into Doye and properties
