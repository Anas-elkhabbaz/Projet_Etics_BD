INTRODUCTION

When a suspect in custody or compelling circumstances invokes his right to counsel

3
4

or to remain silent, all questioning must immediately cease. When that rule is violated—

5

when an officer continues to question a suspect after an invocation of his Miranda rights—

6

7

8

to interrogate Mr. Courtier. This continued interrogation violated both the Oregon and

11

United States Constitutions. All resulting statements by Mr. Courtier, and all evidence

12

Oregon and Federal Law Require Suppression of Involuntary Statements and
Statements Obtained in Violation of Miranda.
Both the Oregon and United States Constitutions provide that no person shall be

“compelled in any criminal prosecution to testify against himself”2 or “compelled in any
criminal case to be a witness against himself.”3
Where, as here, statements of the defendant are challenged as involuntary, the state

7
8

9

10
